Leaving Segovia and driving all the way to the Pyrenees takes a lot out of you, physically and mentally. The way I am, that effort should be rewarded with a route of some alpine substance.

The goal of the trip was the Salenques-Tempestades ridge on Pico Aneto. I'll tell you now that we didn't manage it, unfortunately. We didn't have the gear for the hard winter conditions we found once we saw the route from below. So we went for a ridge with a really fun south flank, which kept us mostly on easy rock without too much snow. The ridge seems to have no name, because its actual prominence is only 6 metres.

## Approach

We reach the La Besurta car park around 8pm and go to bed at 9pm. Lori sleeps in the car, I sleep just outside it, by the door. After 3 hours of very deep sleep we have breakfast and set off at 2am.

The approach to the ridge we wanted to do follows the path towards the Plan d'Aigualluts as far as the waterfall. It then crosses the flat ground and takes the valley to the right of the Tuca d'Aigualluts. It carries on along the river to the Turón de Barrancs, then the path drops and climbs again to the Collado de Salenques, where the route starts.

## Change of plan

Before descending and really committing to the route, the moonlight lets us take a good look at the situation, and it looks quite hard. Patches of snow cover almost the whole ridge. The route is within our technical ability, but its length and how physical it is would already make it nearly a step too far. In drier conditions we would gladly have given it a go. In these it would have exposed us to serious risks. So we decide not to carry on. We turn right, leave the path and continue on a purely exploratory basis towards the Barrancs glacier.

After a short stop behind a recess, out of the wind, to wait for some light, we head onto the snow, which is hard and easy going. We sit down for a second breakfast while we wait for dawn, about twenty minutes from the foot of the route we would later decide to do.

Meanwhile we look around, with the whole amphitheatre of the Aneto massif around us. I spot a ridge running north-east from the summit of Aneto, and the flank facing us (south) looks very doable. I get excited seeing something like a cross on top, which later turns out to be weather equipment. With no phone signal to check the conditions or the difficulty, we walk on, still on an exploratory basis, to the start of the ridge. It's a shoulder, and from its far side you can see the whole Aneto normal route.

## The ascent

Reassured to know exactly where we are, and with our possible escape routes clearly identified, we start up the ridge from the south flank, which avoids the snow-covered slabs on the right.

We carry on alternating between moving together and pitching the slightly more exposed sections. We try to stay left on the south flank and only rarely go along the crest itself. The whole route can be done at grade III, with various options to choose a different line and make it more fun. The last section before the summit, for example, can be taken on a south-facing wall that looks like V+/6a, or you can go round behind it on easy broken rock.

On top there is a weather station, at about 3,050 m. The ridge would carry on, dropping a little and then going to the summit of Pico Aneto over two more steps. Because of the time, though, we decide to downclimb and rejoin the Aneto normal route, which we follow back to the car park.

## Gear

- 5 cams, sizes 0.25 to 2, though we almost only used the 1
- One 50 m rope, more than enough: there are no compulsory abseils and the whole route can be downclimbed
